Fluid on a phone — the rules 375 · 390 · 430 · 768
08 · What flexes, what never does
Never flexesRow height 56px. Tap targets 44px. Hairline 1px. The four type sizes. A phone is not a small desktop — shrinking a control is how a design becomes unusable rather than compact.
FlexesThe gutter, the arc, and the space between sections. Everything else holds its size and lets the container breathe around it.
Gutterclamp(16px, 6vw, 28px) — 22px at 375, 23px at 390, 26px at 430. One expression, no breakpoints.
The arcmin(72vw, 300px). It is the one element allowed to scale with the viewport, because it is the one element that is purely a picture of a number.
The hero numberclamp(64px, 21vw, 92px). Never smaller than 64 — at that size it stops being the hero and the screen loses its subject.
09 · Where the height goes
375 · SE, mini667–812pt tall. The arc drops to 270px and the three-value table loses its top padding. Nothing is removed — a small phone gets the same screen, tighter.
430 · Pro MaxThe extra 90pt goes to the gap ABOVE the action rows, not into the arc. Actions stay anchored to the bottom third where the thumb is; a taller phone must not push them further away.
LandscapeTwo columns: instrument left, rows right. The only layout change in the whole system, and it earns itself — a 390×844 column rotated is unreadable.
768+ · tabletThe column caps at 480px and centres. Do not stretch rows to 700px; a 56px row that wide has its label and its value in different postcodes.
10 · Safe areas — non-negotiable
Bottomenv(safe-area-inset-bottom) plus 14px. The home indicator swallows a bare 20px of padding, and the nav is the thing it swallows.
TopNothing painted in the status bar's space, and no fake status bar drawn. iOS renders the real one on top; a painted one reads as doubled.
KeyboardThe address field on the trip screen scrolls into view above the keyboard rather than the page jumping. dvh, never vh — Safari's toolbar makes vh lie.
11 · Fluid means the touch answers, not that things slide
PressRow background to 5% white in 80ms. Instant enough to feel like the surface, not an animation. No scale, no ripple — a 56px row that shrinks under the thumb is a card pretending to be a button.
CommitThe state label on the right crossfades in 160ms. That is the whole feedback: the row already said BLOCATĂ, now it says DEBLOCATĂ. No toast for something visible on screen.
WaitingThe label goes amber and counts: TRIMIT… 3s. A spinner says something is happening; a counter says how long it has been, which is the question after five seconds.
ArrivingArc sweep 1100ms, rows stagger 120ms — once, on first paint. A returning navigation replays nothing; the screen is already yours.
Number changeTabular digits crossfade in place, 200ms. Never a slot-machine roll — the reading must be legible at every frame, because someone is reading it while walking.
Reduced motionprefers-reduced-motion removes the sweep and the stagger, keeps the crossfades. Feedback is not decoration and never gets cut.
12 · Easy to use, specifically
One thumbEverything you tap lives below 55% of the screen height. The instrument owns the top and is read, not touched.
One tapValues commit on tap — no Apply. The only exception is temperature, where the stepper needs a settling moment, and confirmation, which exists to be slow.
No hidden stateEvery row carries its own state on the right. Nothing requires opening a screen to learn whether it is on.
Refusals say whyA disabled row keeps its reason beside it. "Nu e conectată" is an answer; a greyed button is a shrug.
Scroll is honestNo screen in the set scrolls past two viewports. If it would, it is two screens.
Only glass decides these
Everything above is written to be implementable, not to be admired. Three of these numbers are guesses until the app is open outdoors on a real phone: the 64px hero floor, the 72vw arc, and whether an 8% hairline survives a blue-light filter.

The artboards beside this one are 375 and 430 so the rules are visible rather than asserted — but an artboard is a picture. The next honest step is one screen in code.
